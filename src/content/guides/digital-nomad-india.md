India rewards remote workers who slow down. Pick one or two bases, stay a month or more, and the country gets easier, cheaper and a lot more fun.

## Where to base yourself

- **Goa** for beaches, cafés, a large remote-work crowd from October to May, and quieter, greener monsoon months. Our [Anjuna stay](/stays/goa-anjuna/) sits a short ride from the beach.
- **Himachal** for mountains, cooler summers and focus. [Dharamkot](/stays/dharamkot/) is a fifteen-minute walk above McLeodganj.

## Getting online

Home broadband in India can be fast but is not guaranteed in villages and hill stations. Ask any place you book for its measured speed and whether it has power backup and a second line. Carry a local SIM as a hotspot backup: see [how to buy a SIM card in India](/blog/buy-sim-card-india/).

## Visas

Check current rules on the official [Indian e-Visa site](https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/) before you travel. Our [visa guide](/blog/digital-nomad-visa-india/) explains the options people use and what to prepare.

## Money

UPI, India's instant payment system, is everywhere, from cafés to scooter rentals. Read [UPI payments for foreigners](/blog/upi-payments-foreigners-india/) and carry some cash for places where the network drops.

## Getting around

Trains and overnight buses connect most bases. Inside Goa, a scooter is the simplest way around: here is [how to rent one](/blog/rent-scooter-goa/). Avoid the common [tourist traps](/blog/india-tourist-traps-digital-nomads/).

## Finding your people

Loneliness is the hidden cost of remote work. Coliving with a real events calendar fixes most of it: see what is on in [Happenings](/happenings/), or [make a profile](/village/) and see who will be around when you arrive.
